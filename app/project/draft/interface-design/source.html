The Problem

Small planning decisions are spread across different apps throughout the day.

Each app shows part of the picture, but not enough to make a decision. An email may include a deadline, while the calendar shows available time. Tasks and reminders add more things to handle. Users have to bring these pieces together themselves before they can decide what to do and when.

One sit-down, once a day

Most of these decisions get pushed into one planning session, which can easily take 20–40 minutes.

The Google Calendar Gap → The Opportunity

What already works

Gemini already handles the first step well.

In Gmail, Gemini can recognize event details and offer an “Add to calendar” action. Users can review the details before anything is added.


For a single email, this works well: the information is identified, turned into an event, and confirmed in one place. The gap starts when users need to connect that event with the rest of their day.

The opportunity

Bring these small decisions into one place and let users deal with them as they come up. The queue can include calendar conflicts, reminders, and undated tasks — not just email. AI can suggest where something fits, while the user stays in control of every change.

04

Design Risks & Considerations

Risk

Why it matters

How I address it

The queue feels endless

If there is always another card, users may stop treating it as something they can finish.

Keep the daily queue limited and give it a clear end. Anything left can come back later.

Accepting becomes too easy

A quick swipe is useful, but it also makes it easy to confirm something without checking.

Let users undo changes and review accepted items later.

The suggestion is wrong

A wrong suggestion can make users stop trusting the feature very quickly.

Show where the suggestion came from, and ask when there isn’t enough information.

It interrupts at the wrong time

Even a useful suggestion can be annoying if it appears at a bad moment.

Keep most suggestions in the queue and let users open them when they have time. Only urgent conflicts interrupt.
